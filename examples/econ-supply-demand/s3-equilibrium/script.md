[[both-curves]] Put the two curves on the same axes. Demand slopes down, and supply slopes up.

[[cross]] They cross at one point: one dollar and forty cents a gallon, and six hundred million gallons.

[[too-high]] Suppose the price were higher, say one dollar and eighty cents. Sellers would offer six hundred and eighty million gallons, but buyers would want only five hundred million.

[[surplus]] That gap is a surplus. Unsold gasoline piles up, so sellers cut their prices.

[[too-low]] Now suppose the price were one dollar and twenty cents. Buyers would want seven hundred million gallons, and sellers would offer only five hundred and fifty million.

[[shortage]] That gap is a shortage. Buyers compete for scarce gasoline, so the price climbs.

[[settle]] Either way, the price moves toward the crossing point. There, the plans of buyers and sellers agree.
